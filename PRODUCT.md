# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Fellow frontend and web developers who discover the blog through search, social media, or shared links. They're looking for practical solutions to real problems — not marketing fluff or superficial tutorials. They read to learn something they can apply immediately.

A secondary audience is random visitors evaluating the author's work: potential collaborators, employers, or clients who judge credibility through the quality of writing, code, and design.

## Product Purpose

A public learning journal. Writing to clarify thinking, document discoveries, and share what works. The blog exists to think out loud — each post is a byproduct of building and learning, not content manufactured for an audience. Success is measured by depth and honesty, not traffic.

## Positioning

Practical depth. Real code, real problems, no fluff. A working developer's notebook where every post reinforces that the author actually builds things and writes about them with precision.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: none explicit. The reading experience is the point; a post that earns a bookmark or a share is the win.
- Secondary CTA: browse more posts — deeper engagement with the content, not a funnel.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: Clean code, clean writing, clean design — a developer with genuine attention to detail.
- Belief ladder:
  1. This person knows what they're talking about (proof: demo quality, code quality in the repo itself)
  2. They explain things clearly and practically (proof: post structure, prose style, real examples)
  3. They build real things, not just talk (proof: embedded interactive demos, open source projects)
  4. This is worth returning to (proof: design quality, content depth, signal-to-noise ratio)
- Proof on hand: embedded interactive demos in posts, the blog's own codebase as a quality exhibit, open source projects linked from the homepage

## Brand Personality

Restrained and thoughtful. Quiet confidence, understated. Like a well-edited indie magazine — nothing shouts, everything is deliberate. The design doesn't compete with the content; it frames it.

Three words: precise, understated, crafted.

Reference lane: design-forward personal sites (rauno.me, paco.me) — opinionated typography, minimal chrome, content-first layouts where every element that survives editing earns its place.

## Anti-references

- **SaaS landing page**: gradient heroes, glass cards, icon grids, metric counters, fake urgency
- **Generic tech blog**: the same layout, same tone, same visual language as every other DevRel blog — indistinguishable and forgettable
- **Corporate sterile**: too polished, too safe, no personality. The opposite of a personal site.

## Design Principles

1. **Content first, chrome minimal.** The design serves the writing, not the other way around. Every UI element that isn't the post body must justify its existence.
2. **Earn attention through craft.** Code, writing, and design reflect the same standard. A typo in prose and a 1px misalignment carry equal weight.
3. **Show, don't just tell.** Embedded demos and live code prove the points the prose makes. Abstract explanation without concrete example is incomplete.
4. **Quiet confidence.** No shouting, no flash, no trendy decoration. Understated but unmistakably deliberate. The reader feels the care without being told about it.
5. **Practical over theoretical.** Real problems, real code, real solutions. Every post should leave the reader with something they can use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Good defaults: semantic HTML, reasonable color contrast, keyboard navigable, reduced-motion support for animations. No formal WCAG conformance target, but the craft principle extends to accessibility — broken a11y is broken craft.
